Why I Built This (And Why It Might Help You)

I'm a weekend golfer. I play once or twice a month. Sometimes more, sometimes less. I'm not a member of a club. I don't play in official competitions. I'm just someone who loves the game and wants to know how I'm actually doing.

For years I had a problem that I think a lot of casual golfers in South Africa have:

I wanted a handicap. But I wasn't willing to pay for one.

Not because I don't value what clubs offer — I do. But because paying R2,000 to R15,000 a year for a club membership, just to play twice a month and keep a number, didn't make sense for my situation.

Here's what I found when I looked into it:

The cost of an official handicap

To get an official handicap in South Africa, you need to be a member of a GolfRSA-affiliated golf club. That club has to be registered with GolfRSA, and your scores have to be entered through the HNA (Handicaps Network Africa) system.

That system is good. It's the official system. It works.

But the cost adds up:

  • Club membership: R2,000 to R15,000 per year, depending on the club and package
  • HNA fees: usually included in club fees, but not always
  • Time commitment: most clubs expect you to play, support the club, maybe attend events
  • Extra costs: cart fees, competition entry fees, food and drinks minimums at some clubs

For someone who plays casually — say once or twice a month — that's a lot of money for a number.

The bigger problem: you lose everything when you leave

This is the part that really bugged me.

A friend of mine was a club member for eight years. Played every weekend. Had a solid handicap. Then he moved cities, life got busy, and he let his membership lapse.

He lost his handicap.

Not just the number — everything.

  • His entire scoring history
  • Every round he'd ever entered
  • The ability to track his progress

Because as soon as you stop being a member, your HNA account goes dormant. Your scores stay in the system, but you can't see them. You can't enter new rounds. You can't track anything. You can only get it all back if you rejoin and start paying fees again.

To me, that felt wrong.

The handicap number itself is personal. It represents your game, your improvement, your rounds. Why should it disappear just because you stopped paying a membership fee?

What casual golfers actually want

I think most casual golfers in South Africa want the same thing:

  • To know their handicap. So they can see how they're playing.
  • To play fairly with their friends. So a 5-handicapper and a 25-handicapper can have a competitive match.
  • To track their improvement. To see if the lessons and practice are working.
  • To not pay R5,000+ a year for a number they'll use twice a month.

That's not asking for much. And that's why I built Golf Handicap.

The Official Way: HNA & GolfRSA

Before I tell you about my app, let me be straight with you about the official way to get a handicap in South Africa. Because if you can afford it and you play regularly, joining a club is still the right move.

The official system

In South Africa, the only officially recognised handicap is issued through GolfRSA and managed on the HNA (Handicaps Network Africa) system. To get one, you need to:

  • Join a GolfRSA-affiliated golf club — most clubs in South Africa are affiliated, but not all
  • Pay the club membership fee — usually somewhere between R2,000 and R15,000 per year, depending on the club and package
  • Get registered on the HNA system — most clubs do this for you when you join
  • Wait for your membership to be processed — usually about a week
  • Receive your handicap number and login — you can then enter your scores and track your official handicap

It's a good system. It's the official system. And if you play in club competitions or amateur tournaments, you need it.

What you get with an official handicap

  • ✅ A handicap recognised across South Africa (and internationally through WHS)
  • ✅ Ability to play in club competitions
  • ✅ Ability to enter GolfRSA amateur events
  • ✅ Your scores counted in the official system
  • ✅ Full WHS calculation with all the trimmings — Playing Conditions Calculation, Exceptional Score Reductions, soft caps and hard caps

What you don't get when you leave

Here's the thing I think a lot of golfers don't realise until it's too late:

If you stop being a member, you lose access to your handicap.

Not just the number — everything:

  • Your entire score history stays locked in the HNA system
  • You can't enter new rounds
  • You can't view your old ones
  • You can't calculate anything

Some clubs charge a "dormant member" fee. Some let you keep a non-competing handicap if you pay a smaller annual subscription. But the general rule is: no membership, no handicap.

For a lot of golfers — the ones who play once or twice a month, who just want to know how they're doing — that's a tough pill to swallow.

Should you join a club?

Here's my honest advice:

Play every week? Join a club. You'll get full value for the money.
Play in competitions? Join a club. There's no way around it.
Play 2–4 times a month? Consider a social membership or a cheaper club. Some clubs offer these.
Play once or twice a month? Keep reading. My app is for you.
